# Full Website Evidence Audit and Remediation Prompt

## Role

Act as a senior product engineer, data-quality engineer, editorial researcher, and evidence-provenance auditor.

You are working on the Data Center Pulse website, a MENA/GCC data-center intelligence product with the longer-term goal of covering India and the Middle East.

Your responsibility is not merely to make the site render. Your responsibility is to ensure that every public number, label, insight, claim, score, profile, facility record, and source statement is **accurate, traceable, regionally relevant, and honestly described**.

Work directly in the existing repository and connected Supabase project. Inspect first, create an evidence-based remediation plan, then implement the required changes. Do not invent evidence, capacity, sources, people, events, market numbers, or regional relevance.

---

## Primary objective

Audit the entire website and data pipeline, identify content that is:

- AI-generated but presented as verified
- Generic rather than MENA/GCC-relevant
- Based on one weak source
- Missing a source or evidence excerpt
- Based on stale or unreviewed records
- Displayed with a misleading metric label
- Duplicated across pages
- Using static fallback or illustrative data as if it were live
- Showing empty or loading states that do not describe the real failure
- Showing people, facilities, capacity, lifecycle, or market claims without sufficient proof

Then take the required action:

1. **Keep** content that meets the public evidence policy.
2. **Relabel** content that is useful but only AI-inferred, single-source, or uncorroborated.
3. **Move to review** content that may be valid but needs human approval.
4. **Archive or hide** content that is unsupported, irrelevant, stale, duplicated, or misleading.
5. **Fix the underlying pipeline** so the same problem cannot immediately return.
6. **Update the UI** so the public product clearly communicates evidence level, regional relevance, freshness, and uncertainty.

Do not solve the problem by merely changing wording while leaving false or unsupported data public.

---

## Non-negotiable principles

### 1. AI is not evidence

AI may classify, summarize, extract, group, and propose an insight. AI must not be treated as independent confirmation.

Use separate concepts:

- `ai_inferred`: generated by a model
- `source_validated`: publisher and URL passed source checks
- `claim_validated`: the specific claim passed evidence checks
- `corroborated`: independently supported by another source
- `human_approved`: reviewed by an authorized human

Never use one label to imply another.

### 2. Every public claim must be traceable

For every public claim, the system must be able to show:

- Original source URL
- Source publisher
- Publication date
- Source tier
- Evidence excerpt or structured field
- Last checked date
- Confidence/evidence status
- Corroboration count where relevant

### 3. Regional relevance must be proven

A story is not MENA/GCC-relevant merely because it mentions AI, cloud, chips, storage, or data centers.

Classify content as:

- GCC-specific
- Wider Middle East/MENA
- India-specific
- Global but strategically relevant
- General technology
- Not relevant

Do not call a global hardware story a MENA story unless the article contains a supported regional link.

### 4. Missing data is not zero

Use:

- `Not disclosed`
- `Not yet verified`
- `Source unavailable`
- `Approximate location`
- `Single-source claim`

Never convert missing capacity, coordinates, investment, or dates into zero, certainty, or a precise value.

### 5. Fewer, better records are preferable

Do not preserve content solely to increase counts. A smaller trusted product is better than a large AI-generated directory with weak evidence.

## Phase 4 — evidence and source audit

### News articles

For every published article, audit:

- Source domain
- Source tier
- Source reliability score
- Source URL
- Original publication date
- Publication status
- Validation status
- Validation score
- Source excerpt
- Corroboration count
- Primary source count
- Regional relevance
- Duplicate status
- Freshness

Public publication rules:

```text
Minimum article rule:
valid URL
+ valid publication date
+ approved source or approved specialist source
+ readable source content
+ no duplicate
```

For MENA/GCC-labelled stories additionally require:

```text
regional entity, facility, country, project, policy, or market evidence
```

### Market signals

Do not publicly show a signal unless it has:

```text
at least 2 independent sources
OR 1 primary source + 1 credible specialist source
```

If it has only one source, label it:

```text
Early signal — single source
```

If it has no source, hide it from the public site and retain it only for internal review.

### Regional outlooks

Do not publish a regional outlook for UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman, Kuwait, India, MENA, or any other region unless:

- The region is explicitly connected to the evidence.
- At least one source is available.
- Two sources exist for strong market claims.
- The update date is shown.
- Uncertainty is described.

Remove or hide global regional outlooks if they do not serve the product scope.

### Strategic insights

Each strategic insight must answer:

```text
What changed?
Where?
Who is involved?
What is the evidence?
Why does it matter?
What remains uncertain?
What should the reader watch next?
```

Reject generic statements that could appear on any data-center website.

### Pulse index

Audit:

- Score calculation
- Article universe
- Date window
- Source filtering
- Region filtering
- Sentiment weighting
- Duplicate weighting
- AI involvement
- Confidence tier
- Published versus unpublished inputs
- Source count versus validated-source count

If the score is AI-inferred, do not present it as a market fact. Display:

```text
AI-assisted signal
Based on X published sources
Y corroborated claims
Z MENA-relevant sources
Last updated: date
```

If evidence is insufficient, replace the score with:

```text
Not enough verified evidence for a public index
```

## Phase 6 — leaders and people audit

A person may be public only if:

- The record is genuinely a person.
- The name is confirmed by source text.
- The title is confirmed.
- The organization is confirmed.
- The source is relevant to data centers or infrastructure.
- The mention is not incidental.
- There is at least one source excerpt.

Prefer at least two relevant mentions for a public “leader” badge.

Remove or move to review:

- Organizations represented as people
- Generic authors
- Unrelated political or military names
- People with incidental mentions
- Records with missing title or organization

Use “Mentioned in coverage” instead of “Industry Leader” when the evidence does not support leadership recognition.

## Phase 8 — implement the remediation

Implement the necessary changes in priority order.

### Priority 1 — stop misleading public claims

- Replace incorrect counts.
- Remove unsupported “validated MENA” labels.
- Hide source-free signals.
- Hide zero-source regional outlooks.
- Relabel AI-inferred scores.
- Replace generic empty-state copy.
- Separate global stories from GCC/MENA stories.

### Priority 2 — enforce evidence gates in backend jobs

Update ingestion and insight functions so that:

- Unknown domains are quarantined.
- Only approved sources enter the trusted candidate flow.
- AI outputs retain source article IDs.
- High-impact claims require corroboration or human review.
- Market signals require two sources before public publication.
- Facilities require source-backed lifecycle and capacity values.
- People require title and organization evidence.

### Priority 3 — improve the public UI

Add visible evidence metadata:

```text

```

Use explicit labels:

- AI-assisted
- Specialist-source

- Corroborated

- Not yet verified

### Priority 4 — build an admin review surface

Add or improve an admin research dashboard containing:

- New candidates
- Quarantined domains
- Source health
- Claims awaiting review
- Facilities with changed lifecycle
- Capacity claims
- People awaiting review
- AI insights below evidence threshold
- Failed feeds
- Last successful pipeline run

Every review action must write an audit record.
